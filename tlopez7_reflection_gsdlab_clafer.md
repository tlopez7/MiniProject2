# Reflection: gsdlab_clafer

gsdlab/clafer shows the most dramatic decline of all 10 projects studied: heavy team activity from 2011-2015 is followed by a 50-month gap (2019-06 to 2023-07) -- the longest gap in the entire dataset -- after which only about 1% of all commits occurred. This gap was easy to interpret from the author data: pre-gap commits came from a small team, while post-gap commits come from only one person (Michal Antkiewicz, under a new institutional email), meaning the project's lead researcher returned alone after the rest of the original contributors moved on. The post-gap commits (upgrading to a newer Haskell/GHC toolchain) are purely reactive maintenance to keep the project buildable, not new development -- this looks like an academic research project kept minimally alive by its original lead long after active development ended.
